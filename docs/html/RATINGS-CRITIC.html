§3The dossiersexplicit, per skill
№ 01traceifyabductive · repair loop9.1
The best skill in the set, and the most universally useful — everyone debugs, few people plan.

Every rule here is load-bearing. Capture symptoms before forming hypotheses (kills the jump-to-a-fix reflex). Hypotheses must be falsifiable and ranked by likelihood. Run the cheapest discriminating test first, and stop at the first confirmed cause. The root-cause / trigger / symptom trichotomy is exactly the distinction most debuggers blur. And the single best line in the whole repo: "Do NOT stack workarounds." The trivial / non-trivial fork means a one-line fix never gets dragged through the pipeline.

I put this #1 where the consensus had it tied for fourth, because I weight frequency of use and airtightness over novelty. Debugging is the most common real task an agent faces, and this is the most airtight instruction set of the nine.

Strengths
  • Falsifiable, ranked hypotheses — a real method, not vibes
  • Cheapest-test-first ordering saves real time
  • Root-cause vs trigger vs symptom, made explicit
  • Trivial/non-trivial fork respects task size
Reservations
  • Hypothesis-ranking is a learned skill — juniors get less (J 8.5)
  • Genuinely hard to improve; this is a ceiling, not a floor

№ 02reviewifycritical · judgment9.0
Senior taste, successfully written down — which is harder than it sounds.

The lens-selection heuristic is the star: the two contract lenses (requirement fit, invariant safety) are always on, you pick one or two more from what the diff actually touches, and — crucially — you must state which lenses you skipped and why. That last clause is what keeps the review honest instead of letting it decay into coverage theater. The severity filter is just as good: reject findings that merely restate the linter, naming preferences with no comprehension cost, or rewrites of untouched code. That kills the other great AI-review sin, padding. "If you can't reconstruct intent, that's F1 at Blocking severity" is an excellent forcing function, and the solo/full split is right.

Strengths
  • Deep-on-few beats shallow-on-all-nine
  • "State what you skipped" enforces honesty
  • Severity filter strips linter-noise and preference-findings
  • ADR / glossary bars (three tests each) prevent doc bloat
Reservations
  • Structures judgment but cannot supply it — a tasteless reviewer still produces a bland review
  • Full mode is still a fair amount of ceremony when triggered

№ 03undumbifyconvergent · intent extraction8.8
Contains the single best idea in the repository — and a vocabulary that still scares juniors.

The Materiality Gate is the best idea here: "Would two plausible answers produce materially different, difficult-to-reverse outcomes?" If no, pick the conservative default and move on. That one question kills the number-one failure mode of AI clarification — the twenty-question interrogation that stalls work. Context-awareness (mark each of the six dimensions known / assumed / unknown before asking anything) is the correct refinement, and the six dimensions themselves are well chosen, with plain-language aliases for the user-facing questions.

Why not higher: the vocabulary is senior-oriented. "Feeling of done" and "pushback permission" as named fields will make a junior bounce, even with the escape hatches. It scores 9.5 from the AI and expert seats but only 7.0 from the junior seat — and that drag is real, not cosmetic.

Strengths
  • Materiality Gate kills over-questioning at the root
  • Context-aware: never asks what's already known
  • Anti-examples treated as high-signal — correct
  • Priority ordering resolves conflicts silently
Reservations
  • Field names are senior-coded; junior adoption suffers
  • "Feeling of done" feels forced for purely technical tasks (escape hatch helps)

№ 04shapeifystructural · planning8.6
The right fix, well executed — but its value scales with task size, and the top end is still heavy.

Step 0 (Choose Packet Weight, "when in doubt: lite") is the correct answer to the old "tax form for three files" complaint, and the escalation path — a lite packet that fails twice gets re-shaped as Full — keeps it safe without front-loading the cost. The Revision Request vs Packet Defect distinction (the plan is wrong vs the intent is wrong) is genuinely good design, as are the ISOLATE / BATCH granularity tags that let shipify calibrate validation frequency to risk.

Why I sit just below the consensus: even the lite path asks the agent to correctly self-assess complexity, and the full packet template is still fifteen-plus fields. It's only reached when earned — but when you get there, it's a lot. An excellent planning skill whose payoff is proportional to how big the job already is.

Strengths
  • Lite/Full split with "when in doubt: lite" default gravity
  • Living document — cheap in-place revision, not full re-shape
  • Fact / Assumption / Decision kept distinct
  • Granularity tags hand risk calibration to the executor
Reservations
  • Full packet is 15+ fields — intimidating when reached
  • Relies on honest self-assessment of complexity

№ 05explorifydivergent · option generation8.5
The most beautifully designed skill — and I've docked it for exactly that reason.

"Axes, not variations" is a real insight: blue-button vs green-button is the same axis, but eliminate-the-button vs make-it-a-gesture vs make-it-ambient are genuinely different axes (presence, modality, agency). The mandatory sketch requirement is smart too — abstract options are unjudgeable, so forcing a visceral, feelable artifact per option is the correct move. The pick / remix / reject / expand interaction model is well built.

But I weight usefulness, and divergence is the rarest cognitive mode in real work. Most of the time people roughly know what they want; they need it sharpened (undumbify) or executed (shipify), not exploded into options. Design 9.5, frequency-weighted usefulness 7.5. The consensus's 8.9 rewards the craft; my 8.5 prices in how often you'll actually reach for it.

Strengths
  • "Axes, not variations" prevents cosmetic pseudo-options
  • Mandatory sketch makes options judgeable
  • Remix / expand / reject model respects user reaction
  • Clean handoff brief into undumbify
Reservations
  • Lowest trigger frequency of the nine
  • High craft, lower cumulative value

№ 06shipifydisciplined · execution8.3
Competent and disciplined — and the least novel skill in the set.

The good parts are genuinely good: adaptive granularity with documented overrides, a clean deviation-classification table (local correction vs local defect vs plan defect vs scope opportunity vs destructive surprise), and the excellent rule that two consecutive failures sharing a root cause mean you stop and emit a Revision Request — because the step's premise is wrong, not its implementation. The lean three-line single-agent completion report is the right amount of receipt.

Why it sits here: it's the most upstream-dependent skill — a bad packet produces bad execution no matter how disciplined the runner — and it has the fewest ideas you couldn't have written yourself. It's a very well-written execution checklist. Solid 8.3; the consensus's 8.7 is, to me, slightly generous.

Strengths
  • Deviation classes prevent silent scope creep
  • Double-failure-same-cause → Revision Request is a great circuit breaker
  • Baseline-before-edit discipline
  • Lean report in single-agent, full only for handoff
Reservations
  • Garbage packet in → garbage out; least standalone value
  • Fewest original ideas of the nine

№ 07explainifyteaching · explanation8.1
A great fix on top of a speculative foundation — the consensus rewarded the fix too heavily.

The chat-first default is the cleanest single fix in the repo: "what does this function do?" gets an answer in chat, not an SVG diagram and a knowledge doc. The escalation criteria — three or more modules, the user explicitly asks, or the same module gets asked about again — are specific and falsifiable, with no judgment calls. All of that is genuinely good, and it's why this skill improved the most between versions.

Why I'm a full point below the consensus's 9.0 (which tied it for first): that ranking rewards one excellent fix very heavily. The skill still carries the teaching cluster's two real costs. The pedagogy is unproven — does saving knowledge docs and glossaries actually make anyone understand the codebase better over time, or just produce docs nobody re-reads? And it shares the heaviest dependency footprint in an otherwise zero-dep system: a Node renderer, profiles, progress.json, glossaries. Inline SVG generation also remains genuinely error-prone for an AI executor. Great fix, uncertain base.

Strengths
  • Chat-first default ends artifact-for-its-own-sake
  • Escalation criteria are specific and falsifiable
  • "Read the real code, never the assumption" discipline
  • Docs updated in place — anti-sprawl
Reservations
  • Teaching payoff is a hypothesis, not a result
  • Heaviest dependency footprint of the nine
  • Inline SVG generation is error-prone for executors

№ 08promptifycoaching · prompt craft8.0
I admire the design more than I trust the outcome.

The pivot from XP / streaks / badges to an evidence-based competency map is the right call. "P1 → developing: you led with intent today" is actionable in a way that "+10 XP — Level 2: Tinkerer" never was, and making quick-mode the default means a conciseness teacher is finally concise about it. The no-re-teaching index check (check the lessons index and glossary before committing to a pattern) is smart, and harvesting from the user's real conversations rather than generic tips is a genuinely novel angle.

But this is the most speculative skill in the set on value. The claim is that debriefing your conversations makes you a better prompter — and that is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated result. It also carries the most machinery (Node renderer, ~/.agents/learnings/, profiles, glossaries, lesson files) for the least certain payoff. The consensus's 8.9 rewards the elegance of the skill-map idea; my 8.0 asks whether the teaching actually converts to durable skill before paying full price.

Strengths
  • Competency map > gamification — honest, actionable signal
  • Quick-mode default practices what it preaches
  • Harvests the user's real words, never invented examples
  • No-re-teaching guard respects the learner's time
Reservations
  • Core value (does coaching work?) is unproven
  • Heaviest machinery-to-payoff ratio of the nine
  • Self-assessed evidence — the agent grades its own teaching

№ 09orientifycartographic · cold-start7.9
Not bad — just the one skill you could most easily do without.

There's real quality here: the Quick Orient (a five-line brief for small repos and narrow questions) is a smart addition, the deletion test for suspected-shallow modules is a nice concept, and "landmines named, not defused" is good scope discipline. The completion criterion — a fresh agent reading the brief asks zero orientation questions — is the right bar.

But orientation is the thing a capable agent or a senior developer does anyway, without a formal process: read the README, find the entry point, trace one flow. The skill mostly earns its keep for subagents and true cold-starts, and even then it's the thinnest of the nine. The consensus already had it last at 8.6; I agree it belongs last and think 8.6 is slightly generous. It's the skill with the smallest gap between "use it" and "just do the thing."

Strengths
  • Quick Orient right-sizes small scopes
  • Deletion test is a sharp, teachable heuristic
  • "Name landmines, don't fix them" — clean scope rule
  • One-flow-traced proof keeps the map honest
Reservations
  • Least essential — capable readers orient without it
  • Thinnest content of the nine
  • Value concentrated in a narrow cold-start niche
